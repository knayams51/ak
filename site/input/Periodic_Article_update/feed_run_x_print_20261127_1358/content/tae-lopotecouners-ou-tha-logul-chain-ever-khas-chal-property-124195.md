**Patna** — The Patna high court has rejected the claim of a petitioner to renew the power of attorney in his favour on behalf of an Australian owner/claimant of the historic Morrison Building, a British-era heritage bungalow, located on the caster side of the capital's famous Gandhi Maidan. The order was passed on November 23 and uploaded on HC website on November 25,

The bungalow had shot to limelight when it was shown in internationally-acclaimed movie Gandhi. The case pertains to the Khas Mahal land measuring I bigha 13 kathas, together with a single-storeyed building and two outhouses, purchased by Mrs Blanche Francis from Mrs Amelia Isabella by a registered sale deed dated July 27, 1925. Thereafter, the state government setted the property in favour of Mrs Blanche Francis on September 12, 1971 for a period of thirty years, with a right of renewal.

After the death of Mrs Blanche Francis in 1973, her daughter, Mrs Hazel Morrison, managed the affairs relating to the property.

Citing the 2002 amendment to the Indian Succession Act and the 2025 repeal act, the court clarified that probate is not mandatory for a will executed by Indian Christians and that Sally Gore's testamentary rights cannot be treated as extinguished merely for lack of probate, but she would need to prove her rights.

The court noted that original claimant Sally Gore filed her last affidavit in the case in 2017, and since then she has neither approached a civil court to prove the validity of her will nor renewed the Power of Attomey.

The HC has not delivered a final judgment on the core issue of restoration of possession. and has disposed of the petition.
